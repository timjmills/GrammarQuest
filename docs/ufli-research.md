# UFLI Foundations Research for Grammar Quest Integration

## 1. What is UFLI Foundations?

UFLI Foundations (University of Florida Literacy Institute Foundations) is an explicit and systematic phonics program created by Dr. Holly Lane and Dr. Valentina Contesse at the University of Florida. After more than two years of development and pilot testing, it was released as a comprehensive K-2 foundational reading skills program.

### What Makes It Effective

- **Research-backed results**: A districtwide pilot study (published in *Reading Research Quarterly*, 2025) showed that students who received UFLI instruction gained more than 1.0 standard deviation in early literacy skills. Gains were equivalent to 8 months of additional instruction for kindergarteners and nearly 1.5 years of extra instruction for first graders.
- **Fidelity matters**: Students of teachers who followed the program more closely (teaching lessons in sequence with all listed steps) saw significantly better results than those who deviated.
- **Cumulative and spiral**: Skills are never taught in isolation. Once introduced, they are reviewed repeatedly. For example, when short /i/ is introduced, it appears in review activities more than 300 times across the next 10 lessons.
- **Explicit instruction**: Nothing is left to discovery. Each grapheme-phoneme correspondence is directly taught with scripted teacher language, articulatory gesture descriptions, and guided practice.
- **Connected text integration**: Every lesson culminates in students reading decodable text that uses only the phonics patterns taught so far, building fluency and comprehension from the start.

### Grade-Level Pacing

- **Kindergarten**: Lessons 1-44 (alphabet through digraphs)
- **1st Grade**: Lessons 38B-84 (review of short vowels through long vowel teams), with overlap at lessons 38B-44 allowing review
- **2nd Grade**: Lessons 38B-128 (review through affixes), with the same starting point allowing teachers to review as needed
- Each lesson is designed for **2 school days at 30 minutes per day** (60 minutes total per lesson)

## 2. The UFLI Lesson Structure (8 Steps)

Each UFLI Foundations lesson follows an identical 8-step routine, split across two days:

### Day 1 (Steps 1-5): Review + New Concept Introduction

| Step | Name | Duration | Purpose | What Happens |
|------|------|----------|---------|-------------|
| 1 | **Phonemic Awareness** | ~2-3 min | Develop sound manipulation skills | Students orally blend phonemes into words (e.g., /f/ /i/ /sh/ = "fish") and segment words into phonemes (e.g., "rush" = /r/ /u/ /sh/). No print involved -- purely auditory. |
| 2 | **Visual Drill** | ~5 min | Build grapheme-to-phoneme automaticity | Teacher shows a grapheme card/slide; students say the corresponding phoneme. Format: "M spells /m/." Starts with most recently taught graphemes and works backward through previously taught ones. Quick pace is critical. |
| 3 | **Auditory Drill** | ~5 min | Build phoneme-to-grapheme automaticity (encoding) | Teacher says a phoneme; students repeat the phoneme and write the corresponding grapheme(s) while saying the letter names. This is the reverse of Step 2 -- going from sound to print. |
| 4 | **Blending Drill** | ~5 min | Develop decoding automaticity | Teacher builds words on a "blending board" using only previously taught graphemes. Students read each word chorally: say each sound, then blend to read the whole word. Words use only previously learned patterns. |
| 5 | **New Concept** | ~15 min | Explicitly teach the new grapheme-phoneme correspondence | Teacher introduces the new concept with scripted explanation, articulatory gesture, and grapheme placement on the sound wall. Students practice letter formation, then decode and encode words with the new pattern. Slide deck provides visual supports. |

### Day 2 (Steps 5 Review + Steps 6-8): Application

| Step | Name | Duration | Purpose | What Happens |
|------|------|----------|---------|-------------|
| 5 | **New Concept Review** | ~5 min | Quick review of Day 1's new concept | Brief review of the grapheme-phoneme correspondence introduced on Day 1. |
| 6 | **Word Work** | ~10 min | Apply phonics to word-level reading and spelling | Students blend, segment, and manipulate phonemes in words. May include word sorts, word chains, or building words with letter tiles. Focus on both reading (decoding) and spelling (encoding). |
| 7 | **Irregular Words** | ~6 min | Learn high-frequency words with irregular spellings | Uses the "heart word" approach: identify which parts of the word follow regular patterns and which "tricky" parts must be learned "by heart." Uses sound boxes and orthographic mapping. Only truly irregular parts are memorized -- decodable parts are decoded. |
| 8 | **Connected Text** | ~15 min | Apply skills to reading sentences and passages | Students read decodable sentences and a short decodable passage/book. Text uses only taught phonics patterns plus known irregular words. Builds fluency and comprehension. Teacher may guide discussion of meaning. |

### Key Design Principles in the Lesson Structure

1. **Review before new**: Steps 1-4 always review previously taught material before Step 5 introduces anything new
2. **Encoding and decoding**: Both directions are practiced (reading letters = decoding; writing letters from sounds = encoding)
3. **Word level before text level**: Students practice at the phoneme level, then word level, then connected text level
4. **Cumulative**: All drills pull from the entire pool of previously taught skills, not just the current lesson
5. **Consistent routine**: The same 8 steps every lesson reduces cognitive load and allows students to focus on content rather than procedures

## 3. How UFLI Integrates Phonics with Connected Text

UFLI's approach to connected text (Step 8) is central to its effectiveness:

- **Decodable passages**: Every lesson from Lesson 8 onward includes a decodable passage. These passages use ONLY grapheme-phoneme correspondences that have been explicitly taught, plus any irregular "heart words" that have been introduced.
- **Controlled vocabulary**: Words in the passages are carefully controlled so students can successfully decode them using the skills they have learned. This builds confidence and prevents guessing.
- **Cumulative text complexity**: As more skills are taught, the decodable passages naturally become longer and more complex, with richer vocabulary and sentence structures.
- **Fluency building**: Students read connected text for fluency practice (accurate, automatic reading with appropriate prosody). The UFLI Fluency Support Guide provides additional guidance.
- **Comprehension connection**: Even at early stages, teachers guide brief comprehension discussions about the decodable text, connecting decoding to meaning-making.

### Example from Lesson 45 (sh /sh/)

**Word list**: shack, she, shed, shelf, shell, shift, shin, ship, shock, shop, shot, shut, ash, bash, blush, brush, cash, clash, crash, crush, dash, dish, fish, flash, fresh, gush, hush, lash, lush, mash, mesh, rash, rush, slush, smash, stash, trash, wish

**Example decodable sentences**:
- "She goes to the shop at ten."
- "Are we in a rush to get to the bash?"
- "Jill says she wants to have fish sticks."
- "We went on a ship."
- "Will you shut the trash can lid?"
- "She grabs a snack from the shelf."

Note how these sentences use CVC words, previously taught consonants and short vowels, plus the new "sh" digraph. Heart words (like "the," "to," "we," "says") appear as needed.

## 4. Pedagogical Approaches

### Explicit Instruction
- Every skill is directly taught with teacher modeling, not discovered by students
- Scripted lesson plans ensure consistency and reduce teacher preparation burden
- Clear explanations of articulatory gestures (how to form sounds with the mouth)

### Systematic Sequence
- Skills build logically: single consonants -> short vowels -> CVC words -> digraphs -> VCe -> syllable types -> vowel teams -> diphthongs -> affixes
- Each new skill builds on previously mastered skills
- No skill is assumed; everything is taught explicitly

### Cumulative Practice
- Steps 1-4 of every lesson review previously taught skills
- Visual and auditory drills start with the most recently taught graphemes and work backward
- The blending drill uses only previously taught patterns
- Connected text uses only taught patterns
- This "spiral review" ensures mastery and prevents skill decay

### Multisensory Engagement
- Visual: grapheme cards, slide decks, printed text
- Auditory: phoneme blending and segmentation, teacher modeling sounds
- Kinesthetic/tactile: writing graphemes (auditory drill), letter formation practice, articulatory gestures

### Orthographic Mapping for Irregular Words
- The "heart word" method teaches students to identify which parts of an irregular word are decodable and which parts must be memorized
- This promotes orthographic mapping (the process of storing words in long-term memory for instant recognition)
- Sound boxes are used to visually represent the sound-to-print mapping

## 5. Integrating UFLI Phonics into Grammar Quest's Existing Format

Grammar Quest currently uses a 5-activity format per sentence:
1. **Corrections** (5 errors to find and fix)
2. **POS tagging** (identify parts of speech)
3. **Manipulation** (change a word to create a new sentence)
4. **Vocabulary** (deep exploration of a word)

### How Each Activity Can Serve a Phonics Focus

#### Corrections (5 errors)
**Current**: capitalization, punctuation, grammar, spelling errors
**UFLI integration**: Make spelling errors phonics-focused, targeting the lesson's grapheme-phoneme correspondence:
- For Lesson 45 (sh): misspell "sh" words (e.g., "the fis is in the dish" -> "the fish is in the dish")
- For Lesson 54 (a_e): misspell VCe words (e.g., "I mak a cake" -> "I make a cake")
- Include errors that contrast the target pattern with similar patterns (e.g., "ship" vs "chip" for sh vs ch)
- Keep 1-2 errors as grammar/capitalization/punctuation to maintain Grammar Quest's core mission
- Dedicate 2-3 errors to phonics/spelling patterns aligned with the UFLI lesson

#### POS Tagging
**Current**: Students identify parts of speech by tapping words
**UFLI integration**: This activity naturally works as-is -- it reinforces word recognition and meaning. The key is to ensure target phonics words appear among the POS-tagged words. For example:
- Lesson 45 (sh): "The **ship** [N] can **rush** [V] on the **fresh** [ADJ] water."
- Students must read and recognize the sh-words to tag them correctly, reinforcing decoding

#### Manipulation
**Current**: Change one word to create a new valid sentence
**UFLI integration**: Make the word being changed a target-pattern word, and encourage replacements that also use the target pattern:
- Lesson 45 (sh): "Change 'fish' to a different sh-word." -> "The shop sells fresh **shells**."
- This creates a "word work" experience similar to UFLI Step 6, where students manipulate words with the target pattern
- Suggested replacements should all be decodable words from the lesson's pattern

#### Vocabulary
**Current**: Deep dive into one word with definition, examples, similar words, antonyms, non-examples, sentence starter
**UFLI integration**: Choose vocabulary words that feature the target phonics pattern:
- Lesson 45 (sh): vocabulary word "shelter" -- a place that protects you from weather
- This connects phonics decoding (reading the word) with meaning (understanding the word)
- The vocabulary word's phonics pattern should be highlighted in some way

### Recommended Error Distribution for UFLI Sentences
For each sentence's 5 corrections:
1. **Phonics/spelling error #1**: Target the current lesson's pattern (e.g., "sh" spelled wrong)
2. **Phonics/spelling error #2**: Target a previously taught pattern for cumulative review
3. **Capitalization error**: Maintain Grammar Quest's grammar instruction
4. **Punctuation error**: Maintain Grammar Quest's grammar instruction
5. **Grammar/usage error OR phonics error #3**: Depending on the sentence, either a grammar point or another phonics pattern

## 6. New Features to Consider for a Phonics-Focused Module

### High Priority (Directly aligned with UFLI methodology)

#### A. Phonics Skill Badge/Label
- Display the current UFLI lesson number and skill name prominently (e.g., "Lesson 45: sh /sh/")
- Show the target grapheme-phoneme correspondence with visual support
- This mirrors UFLI's explicit instruction: students always know what they are learning

#### B. Word List Display
- Show a list of 8-12 decodable words for each lesson that use the target pattern
- Words should appear before the sentence activities, similar to UFLI Step 5
- Students could tap words to hear them pronounced (if audio is added)
- Words should be organized by position of the target pattern (initial, medial, final)

#### C. Sound Highlighting in Sentences
- Visually highlight the target phonics pattern within words in the sentence
- For example, in "The **sh**ip can ru**sh** on the fre**sh** water," the "sh" digraph is bolded/colored
- This draws attention to the grapheme-phoneme correspondence in connected text
- Similar to how UFLI slide decks highlight target patterns

#### D. Decodable Text Emphasis
- Ensure ALL words in UFLI module sentences are decodable using skills taught up to that lesson
- Mark any irregular/heart words with a special indicator (e.g., a small heart icon)
- This maintains UFLI's principle of controlled vocabulary in connected text

#### E. Cumulative Review Indicators
- When a sentence includes review of previously taught patterns, note this
- For example: "This sentence practices sh (Lesson 45) and also reviews ch (Lesson 48) and CVC words (Lesson 5)"
- Helps teachers and students understand the cumulative nature of the practice

### Medium Priority (Enhance learning but not strictly UFLI)

#### F. Phoneme Awareness Mini-Activity
- Before the sentence correction activity, include a brief phoneme blending/segmenting task
- Example: "Blend these sounds: /sh/ /i/ /p/ = ?" with answer "ship"
- This mirrors UFLI Step 1 (Phonemic Awareness) and provides a warm-up
- Could be a simple tap-to-reveal activity

#### G. Encoding Practice
- After sentence correction, include a brief spelling/encoding task
- Example: "Write the word that matches: /f/ /r/ /e/ /sh/ = ?" (answer: "fresh")
- This mirrors UFLI Step 3 (Auditory Drill) -- going from sounds to letters
- Could use a simple letter-selection or typing interface

#### H. Word Chains
- Add a word chain activity where students change one sound at a time
- Example: ship -> shop -> chop -> chip -> chin
- This mirrors UFLI Step 6 (Word Work) phoneme manipulation activities
- Each change should involve the target pattern or review patterns

### Lower Priority (Nice-to-have enhancements)

#### I. Heart Word Tracking
- Maintain a running list of taught irregular/heart words
- Show which parts are regular and which are "heart" parts
- Track across lessons as students learn more heart words

#### J. Progress Visualization
- Show progress through the 128-lesson scope and sequence
- Highlight mastered patterns on a visual "sound wall"
- Track which patterns have been practiced and how many times

#### K. Audio Support
- Add audio pronunciation for target words and sentences
- Particularly valuable for ELL students who need to hear correct pronunciation
- Could include minimal pair contrasts (e.g., ship/chip, shin/chin for sh vs ch)

## 7. Mapping 128 Skills to 256 Sentences (2 Per Skill)

### Should Both Sentences Focus on the Same Skill?

**Yes, but with differentiation.** Both sentences for each UFLI lesson should feature the target phonics pattern prominently. However, they should differ in these ways:

#### Sentence A: "Introduction" Sentence
- **Focus**: Heavy emphasis on the NEW phonics skill being taught in this lesson
- **Target pattern density**: 3-4 words featuring the target pattern
- **Vocabulary complexity**: Simpler, more familiar words
- **Sentence length**: Shorter (5-8 words in corrected form)
- **Error types**: 2-3 phonics/spelling errors targeting the new pattern, plus capitalization and punctuation
- **Cumulative review**: Minimal -- mostly the new pattern
- **Example for Lesson 45 (sh)**: "she has a fish in the shop" -> errors in sh-words, capitalization, punctuation

#### Sentence B: "Application" Sentence
- **Focus**: The new phonics skill IN CONTEXT with previously taught patterns
- **Target pattern density**: 2-3 words featuring the target pattern
- **Vocabulary complexity**: Slightly more complex, incorporating review words
- **Sentence length**: Longer (7-12 words in corrected form)
- **Error types**: 1-2 phonics errors targeting the new pattern, 1 phonics error reviewing a previous pattern, plus grammar/capitalization/punctuation
- **Cumulative review**: Intentional -- includes words from previous lessons
- **Example for Lesson 45 (sh)**: "she grabs a fresh snack from the big shelf" -> sh errors plus review of previous patterns

### Design Principles for Sentence Pairs

1. **Controlled vocabulary**: Every word in the sentence should be decodable using skills taught up to and including the current UFLI lesson, OR be a previously taught heart word
2. **Natural language**: Despite vocabulary control, sentences should sound natural and meaningful -- not stilted or artificial
3. **Age-appropriate content**: Topics should be relatable to K-2 students (school, family, animals, play, food, nature)
4. **ELL-friendly**: Use clear, concrete vocabulary. Avoid idioms or culturally specific references that ELL students might not understand
5. **Grammar instruction maintained**: Each sentence should still have genuine grammar/mechanics teaching points (capitalization, punctuation, basic grammar) alongside the phonics focus

### Progression of Sentence Complexity Across 128 Lessons

| Lesson Range | Section | Sentence Length | Vocabulary | Patterns Available |
|-------------|---------|----------------|------------|-------------------|
| 1-10 | Early Alphabet | 3-5 words | VC/CVC only | Single consonants + short a, i |
| 11-34 | Full Alphabet | 4-6 words | CVC words | All single consonants + all short vowels |
| 35-53 | Review + Digraphs | 5-8 words | CVC + digraph words | Short vowels + digraphs + FLSZ rule |
| 54-62 | VCe | 6-9 words | CVC + VCe words | All above + VCe patterns |
| 63-68 | Longer Words | 7-10 words | Multi-syllable words | All above + suffixes (-es, -ed, -ing) |
| 69-83 | Ending Patterns + R-Controlled | 7-10 words | More complex words | All above + ending patterns + r-controlled |
| 84-97 | Vowel Teams + Diphthongs | 8-12 words | Rich vocabulary | All above + vowel teams + diphthongs |
| 98-128 | Silent Letters + Affixes | 8-12 words | Grade-level vocabulary | Full phonics toolkit + morphology |

### Special Considerations for Early Lessons (1-10)

Lessons 1-4 teach individual letter sounds (a, m, s, t) before students can read words. Lessons 5+ introduce VC and CVC words. For very early lessons:
- Sentences will necessarily be very short and simple
- Heart words (the, a, I, is) will be needed even in the earliest sentences
- Focus on CVC words that use only the letters taught so far
- Example for Lesson 5 (VC & CVC Words, knowing a, m, s, t): "I sat on a mat." (uses /s/, /a/, /t/, /m/ -- all taught letters)
- Example for Lesson 10 (CVC Practice a, i; knowing a, m, s, t, p, f, i, n): "The fat pig sat in a pit." (uses only taught letters)

### Review Lessons (e.g., Lessons 10, 19, 38, 41, 49, 53, etc.)

Review lessons should:
- Include words from ALL patterns covered in that review section
- Have slightly higher target-pattern density since multiple patterns are being reviewed
- Feature a mix of words from different lessons in the reviewed section
- Error types should span multiple previously taught patterns

## 8. Summary of Recommendations for Grammar Quest UFLI Module

### Data Structure Additions

Each UFLI sentence should include, in addition to the existing Grammar Quest fields:

```json
{
  "ufliLesson": 45,
  "ufliSkill": "sh /sh/",
  "ufliSection": "Alphabet Review & Longer Words",
  "phonicsWords": ["ship", "shop", "fish", "fresh", "shelf"],
  "heartWords": ["the", "to", "she", "says"],
  "sentenceType": "intro",
  "reviewPatterns": ["CVC", "short vowels"],
  "decodableLevel": 45
}
```

### Module Organization

- **128 lessons** mapped to the UFLI scope and sequence
- **256 sentences** (2 per lesson): intro + application
- Organized into **14 sections** matching UFLI's groupings (Alphabet, Alphabet Review & Longer Words, VCe, etc.)
- Each section could be presented as a "unit" in the Grammar Quest UI

### Core Principle

The UFLI module should feel like Grammar Quest (same 5-activity format, same engaging UI) but with every sentence carefully constructed to reinforce specific phonics skills in a systematic, cumulative sequence. The grammar correction activities become a vehicle for phonics practice in connected text -- which is exactly what UFLI Step 8 (Connected Text) aims to achieve.
