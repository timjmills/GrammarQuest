# K-5 Grammar and English Language Mechanics Standards: A Comprehensive Cross-Referenced Guide

This document synthesizes the Common Core State Standards (CCSS) Language strand, IXL ELA skill lists, Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills (TEKS), and scope-and-sequence frameworks from major curriculum publishers into one unified, grade-by-grade reference for grammar and English language mechanics instruction. It is designed for curriculum development at an international school serving English Language Learners, with granular skill breakdowns organized by domain within each grade level. Sources cross-referenced include the official CCSS (L.K.1-L.5.6), IXL Learning skill catalogs, Texas TEKS SS110.2-SS110.7, McGraw-Hill Wonders, Easy Grammar Systems, Saxon Grammar, the LETRS word-study progression, the Louisiana DOE Grammar Guide, and the WIDA ELD Standards Framework.

## A note on ELL considerations for international schools

Before diving into the grade-level standards, several cross-cutting principles are essential for ELL populations. Articles (a, an, the) require explicit, sustained instruction because many home languages lack equivalent structures. English SVO word order and pre-nominal adjective placement must be taught directly, since word order varies widely across languages. Verb tense systems differ dramatically -- systematic, explicit instruction in English tenses is critical. The WIDA ELD Standards Framework recommends differentiating grammar targets across word/phrase, sentence, and discourse dimensions. Social language (BICS) develops in roughly two years, but academic language proficiency (CALP) requires five to seven or more years. Sentence frames, mentor sentences from grade-level texts, and visual supports at every grammar exercise level are strongly recommended by both the Louisiana DOE Grammar Guide and EL Education curricula.

For newcomer ELLs, the recommended grammar teaching sequence (from Raise the Bar Reading) is: personal pronouns -> verb "to be" -> verbs vs. nouns -> present tense -> singular/plural nouns -> articles -> demonstratives -> possessives -> present progressive -> negatives -> questions -> adjectives -> comparatives/superlatives -> quantifiers -> past tense -> future tense -> modals -> adverbs -> conjunctions -> prepositions.

## KINDERGARTEN

### Parts of speech

**Nouns.** Use frequently occurring nouns (CCSS L.K.1b) to name familiar people, animals, places, and things, both orally and in writing. Identify whether a noun names a person, animal, place, or thing (IXL). Choose the singular or plural noun that matches a picture (IXL). Form regular plural nouns orally by adding /s/ or /es/ (e.g., dog/dogs, wish/wishes) (CCSS L.K.1c). TEKS K requires editing for singular and plural nouns.

**Verbs.** Use frequently occurring verbs (CCSS L.K.1b). Find action verbs with and without images (IXL). Complete sentences with action verbs to match pictures (IXL). Discuss present vs. past tense orally. TEKS K requires editing for verbs in drafts.

**Adjectives.** Use describing and sensory words orally -- size, shape, color, number -- though the term "adjective" is not required at this level. Compare pictures using adjectives (IXL). TEKS K requires editing for adjectives, including articles.

**Pronouns.** Use simple pronouns correctly in oral language (I, me, he, she, it, we, they). TEKS K includes subjective, objective, and possessive pronoun cases.

**Prepositions.** Use the most frequently occurring prepositions: to, from, in, out, on, off, for, of, by, with (CCSS L.K.1e). TEKS K includes prepositions in the conventions editing standard.

**Question words.** Understand and use interrogatives -- who, what, where, when, why, how (CCSS L.K.1d). Identify which question word applies in context (IXL).

### Sentence structure

Produce and expand complete sentences in shared language activities (CCSS L.K.1f). Speak in complete sentences. Identify telling sentences vs. asking sentences (IXL). Identify and use end marks to distinguish sentence types (IXL). Find complete sentences among options (IXL). Unscramble words to make a complete sentence (IXL). Recognize that words are separated by spaces. Write from left to right, top to bottom. TEKS K requires editing for complete sentences.

### Capitalization

Capitalize the first word in a sentence (CCSS L.K.2a; IXL). Capitalize the pronoun "I" (CCSS L.K.2a; IXL). Capitalize names of familiar people (TEKS K).

### Punctuation

Recognize and name end punctuation -- period, question mark, exclamation point (CCSS L.K.2b). Use periods to end declarative sentences (TEKS K). With support, use question marks and exclamation points.

### Spelling patterns and rules

Write a letter or letters for most consonant and short-vowel sounds (CCSS L.K.2c). Spell simple words phonetically, drawing on sound-letter relationships (CCSS L.K.2d). Print many upper- and lowercase letters (CCSS L.K.1a). Predictable consonants: m, s, t, l, p, f, c (/k/), n, b, r, j, k, v, g (/g/), w, d, h, y, z, x. Short vowels: a, i, o, u, e. CVC words (consonant-vowel-consonant). Long vowel sounds with single letters (me, he, we, so, no, hi). High-frequency sight words (the, a, is, and, etc.). TEKS K specifies spelling words with VC, CVC, and CCVC patterns; spelling with sound-spelling patterns; and spelling high-frequency words from a research-based list. TEKS K also requires forming all uppercase and lowercase letters with appropriate directionality.

### Usage

Form regular plural nouns orally by adding /s/ or /es/ (CCSS L.K.1c). Use plural nouns with -s and -es orally and attempt in writing. Discuss differences between present and past tense verbs orally.

### Vocabulary and word study

Identify new meanings for familiar words and apply them accurately (e.g., duck as a bird vs. the verb to duck) (CCSS L.K.4a). Use the most frequently occurring inflections and affixes (-ed, -s, re-, un-, pre-, -ful, -less) as clues to meaning (CCSS L.K.4b). Sort common objects into categories to understand concepts (CCSS L.K.5a; IXL). Demonstrate understanding of verbs and adjectives by relating them to their opposites/antonyms (CCSS L.K.5b). Match antonyms and synonyms to pictures (IXL). Identify real-life connections between words and their use (CCSS L.K.5c). Distinguish shades of meaning among verbs describing the same action -- walk, march, strut, prance -- by acting out meanings (CCSS L.K.5d). Identify multiple-meaning words with pictures (IXL). Use color and number words (IXL). Use location words -- inside/outside, above/below, next to/beside (IXL). Use words and phrases acquired through conversations, reading, and being read to (CCSS L.K.6).
